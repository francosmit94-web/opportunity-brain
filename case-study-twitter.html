In October 2022, Elon Musk acquired Twitter (now X) for $44 billion. It was one of the most debated tech acquisitions in history. This analysis evaluates the decision using the Opportunity Brain's 7-pillar framework — and reveals what the framework would have predicted before the deal closed.

7-Pillar Scorecard

Market Demand — 6/10

Weight: 50%

238 million daily active users in a $200B+ social media ad market. But Twitter was consistently the smallest major platform with flat growth (5-15% vs TikTok's 40%+). Never profitable on a sustained GAAP basis.

Unit Economics — 3/10

Weight: 50%

$44B purchase price with $13B in debt at ~10% interest = $1.3B/year obligation. 9x revenue multiple for a company with negative margins. The debt alone required doubling revenue or cutting costs by 25%+.

Defensibility — 4/10

Weight: 50%

Strong brand and network effects, but switching costs were low — Threads, Bluesky, and Mastodon emerged within months. Network effects weaker than Meta's, which had 3x the users.

Founder Fit — 7/10

Weight: 50%

Musk had a proven turnaround track record (Tesla, SpaceX). But social media requires different skills than automotive. His controversies alienated advertisers — ad revenue dropped ~50% in year one.

Timing — 2/10

Weight: 50%

Low interest rates made debt accessible, but rates were about to rise. Advertising market was softening. TikTok was eating engagement. "Free speech" positioning alienated the advertiser base.

Capital Efficiency — 2/10

Weight: 50%

The deal's weakest pillar. $13B debt at 10%+ interest. Required selling ~$20B in Tesla stock. Ad revenue dropped 50% post-acquisition. By late 2023, valuation dropped to ~$15-20B — a 55-65% loss.

Exit Potential — 3/10

Weight: 50%

LBO structure made traditional exits difficult. IPO above $44B breakeven unlikely. Most likely outcomes: debt restructuring, distressed sale, or long-term private ownership at reduced valuation.
